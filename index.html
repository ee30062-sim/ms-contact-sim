Forming the Contact

Metal (W) and n-Si are wired together, so the Fermi levels are aligned; a vacuum gap separates them. As the gap closes, the work-function difference moves from the gap into the semiconductor as band bending.

Sweeping the Metal Work Function (bands + J–V)

n-Si, N_d = 10^16 cm^-3. Below phi_s = 4.22 V electrons accumulate and the J–V curve is a straight line (ohmic); above it a barrier phi_B0 = phi_m - chi appears, the contact rectifies and the turn-on voltage moves up. Ideal Schottky–Mott model, thermionic emission, R_s = 5 mOhm cm^2.

Forward and Reverse Bias

The flow from the metal (over phi_B0) stays constant; the flow from the semiconductor changes exponentially with V_a.

"Measuring" C–V

Reverse-bias sweep: charge, field and bands, and the (1/C')^2 plot building up. The straight-line fit returns N_d and V_bi.

Barrier Lowering under Reverse Bias

Image-force lowering grows with the field, so the reverse current does not saturate.

Doping Sweep and Contact Resistance

The barrier height stays, but W ~ 1/sqrt(N_d): above about 10^19 cm^-3 electrons tunnel and R_c collapses (R_c from an interpolation model).
