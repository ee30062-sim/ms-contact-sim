What to notice
  • The metal-side barrier qφB0 does not move with bias; only the semiconductor-side barrier q(Vbi − Va) does. That is why Js→m grows exponentially in forward bias while Jm→s = JsT stays fixed (reverse current saturates).
  • A Schottky diode is a majority-carrier device: current is thermionic emission over the barrier, no minority-carrier storage → fast switching.
  • JsT ≫ Js of a pn diode (tick “compare”): the same current is reached at a much lower turn-on voltage.
  • With image-force lowering on, Δφ ∝ √Emax grows with reverse bias, so the reverse current creeps up instead of saturating (semilog view).
  • Heavy doping makes W only a few nm: E00 becomes comparable to kT and electrons tunnel through the barrier → low Rc ohmic contact.
  • With real metals on n-Si the ideal non-rectifying case is essentially unavailable — practical ohmic contacts rely on heavy doping and tunnelling.
